The Compounding Wiki — Why Knowledge Decays

By Jane Doe, May 2026

Most knowledge bases shrink in usefulness over time. The pages get written once and never revisited. Within twelve months, half the content is stale, contradicted, or simply wrong — and nobody knows which half.

A compounding wiki inverts this. Every new ingest improves the value of what came before. Cross-page synthesis surfaces patterns that weren't visible in the source documents. Lint catches contradictions. Archive moves dead branches out of the way without breaking backlinks.

Three load-bearing patterns

  1. Strict-schema retry. Validate every page against a Pydantic schema; on failure, feed the errors back to the agent and let it retry. Three attempts, then land as draft.
  2. Two-step ingest. Split analyze (cacheable, cheap) from synthesize (state-dependent, expensive). Iterate on prompts cheaply.
  3. Cache-version discipline. Bump the cache version whenever you edit a prompt. Otherwise old cached responses serve against the new prompt — an invisible bug.
